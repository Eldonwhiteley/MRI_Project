**Team Charter**  
A Team Charter defines your goals and documents your team's overall agreements regarding how the team will function. Completing the team charter helps your team come to a shared understanding (shared mental model) of how the teamwork will proceed and what everyone’s role is in supporting and contributing to the team.

### **Communications**

Communication is a pillar to strong teamwork.  Consider the details of how your team will communicate throughout the two semesters in a way that sets you up for success.

* How will you communicate together?  Some examples include email, Google Chat, texting, telephone, etc.   
* Consider the details of the team communication.  
  * Is there a certain time of day that communications relating to the team and project should take place?  
  * What is a reasonable expectation for a response from team members?  
* In-person team meetings are recommended on a project of this scale.  Is there a standard meeting time that would work for everyone, or would you meet only on an “as needed” basis?  How do these meetings get arranged, and how do they take place?  
* How will you handle internal team meetings/communication differently than meetings with your sponsor or professor?  There should be one primary contact between the team and the sponsor

### **Conflict Recognition and Resolution**

Differences of opinion among project team members are common, healthy and should be expected. Because of this, the question here is not how you will avoid these differences, but how you will handle them in order to accomplish your project goals. Think about what you will do to:

* Recognize and openly acknowledge disagreement if it occurs.  This is normal because it’s natural that your opinions will differ from time to time, especially as team members get to know one another and begin working together on the project.  
* Work to resolve any conflict that occurs during the semester in a healthy way.  
* What will be done when an impasse occurs?  
* What will you do if someone isn't pulling their weight, consistently falls behind, etc. This stuff happens for all kinds of fully acceptable reasons, but the team needs to have a way for it to be handled.

  * Find ways to address these issues internally prior to involving your professor.  Positive encouragement, such as facilitating a slacking team member’s involvement or implementing a buddy system, often works better than negative reinforcement.
